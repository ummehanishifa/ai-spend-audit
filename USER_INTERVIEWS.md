# User Interviews

Three interviews conducted via WhatsApp chat, May 2026.

---

## Interview 1 — College Student (CS)

**Name:** Anonymous (requested privacy)
**Role:** Final year CSE student
**Company stage:** Individual user / student

**What AI tools do you use?**
"Probably ChatGPT the most. For coding I use Cursor sometimes and Copilot in VS Code. Perplexity for quick research."

**Do you track your spending?**
"Honestly not really. I just pay for one tool at a time if I actually need it."

**Have you felt like you overpaid?**
"Yeah definitely. Sometimes I buy a tool thinking I'll use it daily, then after one week I go back to using just ChatGPT and forget the other subscription even exists."

**Would you use an audit tool?**
"Honestly yeah, I probably would. Especially because now there are too many AI subscriptions and half of them do similar things anyway. If something could compare them and tell me 'bro you're paying for the same feature twice' that'd actually be useful."

**What would make you trust it?**
"Probably if it showed actual comparisons instead of just randomly promoting tools. Like pricing, features, what I actually use vs don't use, and real user reviews."

**Most surprising thing they said:**
They said they would only share results on social media "if the results looked cool or surprisingly accurate" — this told me the shareable URL needs to look visually impressive, not just functional.

**What it changed about my design:**
Added a visual hero section with big green savings numbers specifically so it looks good when screenshotted and shared.

## Interview 2 — Junior Developer (Fresher)

**Name:** Anonymous
**Role:** Junior Software Developer
**Company stage:** Early career, joined a tech company a few months ago

**What AI tools do you use?**
"Mostly ChatGPT and Copilot right now. At work I use Copilot while coding because it saves time for repetitive stuff. ChatGPT is more for debugging, understanding code, writing mails/docs."

**Does your company pay for tools?**
"Company gives access to Copilot at work, so I don't pay for that separately."

**Do you track your AI spend?**
"Honestly I don't track it exactly. Right now it's mainly just one subscription, so it's manageable. If I start adding more AI tools then it'll probably get expensive fast."

**Have you felt like you overpaid?**
"Yeah, a few times. Like I bought or upgraded tools thinking I'd use them a lot, but then ended up sticking mostly to ChatGPT + Copilot anyway. Felt like I was paying for 'backup options' I barely opened."

**Would you use an audit tool?**
"Yeah I would actually use it. Because in real life as a junior dev, I already feel like I'm juggling too many tools + subscriptions. If something could clearly show me 'you're paying for this but using it 2% of the time' and suggest cheaper alternatives, I'd definitely try it. Saving even a small amount monthly feels worth it when you're just starting out."

**What would make you trust it?**
"If it shows what I actually use vs what I don't, clear cost breakdown, real alternatives with same features. Otherwise I'd just ignore it like most money-saving apps."

**Most surprising thing they said:**
"Saving even a small amount monthly feels worth it when you're just starting out." — I expected junior devs to not care much about costs, but tight budgets make them MORE price-sensitive than seniors.

**What it changed about my design:**
Made the savings display prominent even for small amounts like $10-20/mo — originally I only highlighted large savings.

## Interview 3 — Senior Developer

**Name:** Anonymous
**Role:** Senior Software Developer
**Company stage:** Established tech company, several years experience

**What AI tools do you use?**
"I mostly use ChatGPT for quick problem solving, architecture thinking, and debugging. GitHub Copilot integrated directly into my IDE for day-to-day coding speed. Occasionally Cursor for side projects."

**How has your tool usage changed over time?**
"Over time I've reduced tools rather than increased them. In real jobs, stability and integration matters more than chasing every new AI app."

**Do you track your spending?**
"I don't track it super precisely month to month. Earlier I used to stack tools and then forget I even had some of them running — that's where I definitely felt I was wasting money."

**Have you felt like you overpaid?**
"Early on in my career I did feel that a lot. I'd subscribe to a tool because it looked powerful, use it heavily for a week, and then slowly drift back to my usual setup. After a while I started asking myself: 'Is this saving me time every week, or just looking useful?' Most tools didn't survive that question."

**Would you use an audit tool?**
"Yeah, I would actually use it. Especially if it helps me catch duplicate subscriptions — very common — tools I stopped using but still paying for, and cheaper alternatives that don't reduce productivity."

**What would make you trust it?**
"I'd trust it if it behaves like a serious engineering tool, not a marketing app. Transparent breakdown of how it calculated usage. No hype recommendations — just practical alternatives. As a senior dev, I've seen too many 'AI optimizer' tools that exaggerate savings, so trust comes from data and transparency, not claims."

**Most surprising thing they said:**
"In workplaces, usefulness spreads fast — hype doesn't." — This completely reframed how I think about the viral loop. The shareable URL works not because it looks flashy, but because it provides genuine value that people want to pass to teammates.

**What it changed about my design:**
Added honest messaging — "You are spending well" for optimal cases instead of manufacturing fake savings. Credibility matters more than excitement.

## Key Themes Across All 3 Interviews

1. **Nobody tracks their AI spend precisely** — all 3 said they don't know their exact monthly cost
2. **"Subscription drift" is real** — all 3 described paying for tools they stopped using
3. **Trust comes from transparency** — all 3 said they'd trust recommendations backed by real data and comparisons, not hype
4. **Sharing happens in private channels first** — Slack, WhatsApp, team chats before social media
5. **Junior devs are MORE price sensitive** — surprising finding that changed the design
